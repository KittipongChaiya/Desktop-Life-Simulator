# Phase 55 — Crops & The Seasonal Choice

> **Delivers:** eight new crops taking the table from four to twelve, every
> season from two-or-three plantable to five-or-six, and the art for all of it.
> ADR-046 R-01 and R-02 turn from reported to enforced.
> **Governing decisions:** ADR-046 §2 (the rules), §4 (measurement-driven
> balance); `GAME_DESIGN.md` §3.2 (longer crops pay better — untouched);
> ADR-041 (the drawing vocabulary); ADR-004 §5 (definitions are data).
> **Schema:** none.
> **Status:** **Complete.**

---

## The problem, stated precisely

Four crops cannot fill four seasons. Turnip grows year-round and each of the
other three covers two adjacent seasons, so **spring and winter offered two
plantable crops and summer and autumn offered three.**

The scoping note for v0.6 said something stronger than that and was wrong twice
— it claimed two in _every_ season, and it claimed the crops in a season were
"strictly ordered," meaning one is simply better. The dominance check written
for R-02 disagrees and **passes on the v0.5 content**: turnip is cheap and fast,
pumpkin is expensive and slow and pays far more per visit, and neither beats the
other on all three axes R-02 measures.

So the defect this phase fixes is **volume, not domination** — and that
distinction decided what to author. Twelve crops in a neat power ordering would
have satisfied R-01 and re-created the problem R-02 exists to prevent.

## The constraint that shaped every number

`GAME_DESIGN.md` §3.2 is the single most important balance decision in the game:

> Longer crops yield strictly better coins-per-second. This is the opposite of
> most active games... **it makes going away the optimal strategy.**

The obvious way to make a season interesting is to let long crops pay a rate
penalty in exchange for convenience, which is how most idle games build a
trade-off. **That is §3.2 inverted**, and §3.2 is `VISION.md` §2.2 written as
arithmetic. It was not touched.

**So the prices are computed, not chosen.** Fitting the four existing crops
gives

```
profit/sec  =  0.078 × (seconds / 90) ^ 0.23
```

which reproduces every existing point to within a few percent. Each new crop
declares a growth time and a seed cost; its sale price is whatever that curve
requires. The result holds §3.2 across all twelve crops rather than merely
inside the new ones.

## What a seasonal choice can be, then

If §3.2 permanently fixes which crop earns more, the choice cannot be about
rate. **The axis is capital** — which §3.2 already names as the counterweight:
_"pumpkin seeds cost 12× turnip seeds... progression is about affording
patience."_

**Leek and wheat are that stated as plainly as it can be.** Identical growth
time, identical profit per second to three decimals, half the seed cost, a lower
sale price. A player with six coins plants leeks. A player with twelve plants
wheat and earns more per trip to the market for the same time. Neither is
better; they are for different moments.

| Season | Before | After | Plantable                                       |
| ------ | ------ | ----- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Spring | 2      | **6** | turnip, pea, strawberry, leek, wheat, flax      |
| Summer | 3      | **6** | turnip, strawberry, wheat, tomato, carrot, corn |
| Autumn | 3      | **6** | turnip, flax, carrot, corn, cabbage, pumpkin    |
| Winter | 2      | **5** | turnip, leek, cabbage, squash, pumpkin          |

R-01's floor is three. Zero crops are strictly dominated in any season (R-02).

## Two crops carry more than their own weight

**Flax** is tagged `fibre` and **corn** is tagged `grain`, which is what phase
56's chains ask for. `crops.ts` says tags exist so "v0.3 contracts can ask for a
root vegetable and v0.4 recipes for a grain without either editing this
interface" — this is the first time a second grain has existed for that sentence
to be true about.

## The art, and what the review gate caught

48 sprites: four growth stages, a produce icon and a seed pouch for each of the
eight. Generated by `scripts/generate-crop-art-v06.mjs`, with the shared
vocabulary lifted into `scripts/lib/crop-craft.mjs` so both crop generators draw
with the same hand — a crop drawn in a different vocabulary reads as a different
game, which is the finding ADR-041 opens with. The v0.1 four regenerate
byte-identical; `art-regeneration.test.ts` is what proves that.

A second generator rather than a longer first one, because `CODE_STYLE.md` caps
a file at 800 lines and `generate-crop-art.mjs` is already 696 for four crops.

**Three crops and two icons were redrawn after the contact sheet showed them
failing**, and this is the phase's clearest argument for that gate existing:

| Caught                                                                                                | Fix                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pea's trellis was invisible** — the canes drew first and the leaf mass covered them, leaving a blob | Foliage first, canes over it, standing taller, with a cross-tie at the top |
| **Tomato was a bigger strawberry** — same low leaf mass, same pink fruit at soil level, same colour   | Staked and tall; fruit at mid-height where a strawberry never has any      |
| **Cabbage was a green dome** indistinguishable from the pea's leaf mass                               | A tight head sitting on a wide dark skirt of outer leaves                  |
| **The tomato ICON was the strawberry icon** — one round pink fruit under a green calyx, at 16×16      | A truss of **two** fruits on a shared stem, a silhouette nothing else has  |
| Strawberry's taper was too subtle to carry the difference alone                                       | Exaggerated past life so the outline reads at 1×                           |

None of these was detectable by any test. `sprite-keys.test.ts` proves a key
resolves to a frame; nothing proves the frame is a picture of the right thing.
That is what `generate-contact-sheet.mjs` is for, and it is AI-observable
evidence in ADR-040's sense — I looked at the sheet — rather than machine
evidence.

## Tests

- **`tests/content-census.test.ts`** — R-01 and R-02 are now enforced, because
  `PLAN.md` §0 marks phase 55 COMPLETE. Marking it complete without satisfying
  them fails the suite; that is the gate, and it was verified by inversion at
  phase 54.
- **`tests/crop-curve.test.ts`** (new) — §3.2 itself, asserted for the first
  time in six versions. It held by inspection with four crops on one ladder; it
  will not keep holding by inspection with twelve, and the failure is silent —
  a crop priced slightly wrong makes patience mildly suboptimal and no test
  notices. Three properties: a longer crop never earns a worse rate; the spread
  between the ends stays above 1.5×; and a better rate always costs more to
  plant, which is §3.2's counterweight.

It deliberately pins **no price**. ADR-046 §4 delegates balance for v0.6, and
pinning numbers would turn every legitimate tuning pass into a test edit — which
is how a guard becomes a nuisance and then becomes a `.skip`.

## What this phase did not do

**It did not touch a single existing number.** Every v0.1 crop keeps its growth
time, seed cost and price. The authority to retune exists (ADR-046 §4) and
nothing has yet been measured that requires using it — phase 61 is where the arc
is re-run against the full content set and where that authority gets its
evidence.

**It did not add a system.** No new field, no new command, no save change. A
crop is a data literal plus a generator entry, which is what ADR-046 §1 claimed
and this phase is the first test of.
