# LORE_BIBLE

> **Status:** Authoritative for the fiction. The canonical source every future session obeys when generating stories, NPCs, quests, locations, dialogue, items, monsters, and world-building.
> **Owns:** The narrative canon — world philosophy and themes, timeline and history, the origins of farming and the villages, natural laws, beliefs, festivals and traditions, food culture, the _fiction_ of the economy and trade, famous families, legendary creatures and the reserved mythology of monsters/bosses, magic (as story), and future story hooks.
> **Does not own:** Product tone and intent, which this _serves_ and cites (`VISION.md`); economic and gameplay _numbers_ (`GAME_DESIGN.md §6`); the world's _visual_ canon (`WORLD_BIBLE.md`); how characters _look_ (`CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`); the roadmap that schedules content (`VISION.md §4`, `PLAN.md`).

**Boundary note.** `VISION.md` owns _what the game should feel like_; this file turns that feeling into a place with a past. `GAME_DESIGN.md` owns the coins and crops as _mechanics_; this owns the _story_ around them (why there is a market, who the merchant is). `WORLD_BIBLE.md` owns how the valley _looks_; this owns what it _means_. When the fiction here would contradict a mechanic or the product tone, those win and the fiction bends around them.

**On how much to invent.** This bible is written **deliberately light and open.** Over-specified lore is a contradiction trap: the more that is fixed now, the more a future session can accidentally break. So this establishes a warm, low-stakes present, a faded and half-forgotten past, and dormant mysteries in the far places — enough hooks for the RPG content of v1.0 without locking specifics that would box it in. **What this file does not state is not "missing" — it is open for a future session to establish, at which point that addition becomes canon (§ Consistency).**

---

## 1. World philosophy

The world is a **kind one.** Its deepest premise, matching the product (`VISION.md §2.2`), is that the land _wants_ to give: seeds sprout, seasons turn, and effort is rewarded. Neglect is forgiven; nothing at home is ever lost to inattention. This is not naïveté — the world has old sorrows and far dangers — but its everyday character is warmth, patience, and quiet hope.

If a single sentence must guide every story written in this world: **"A gentle place, an open horizon, and time enough to grow into it."**

---

## 2. Core themes

Every story, quest, and NPC should sound at least one of these:

1. **Growth and patience.** The reward is for tending, not rushing (the crop curve, `GAME_DESIGN.md §3.2`, is this theme as arithmetic).
2. **Belonging and home.** The valley welcomes you; you make a place in it. _Welcome back_ is the emotional keynote (`ART_DIRECTION.md §3`).
3. **Gentle mystery.** Wonder without dread — old ruins, a legend, a light in the woods — that invites curiosity, not fear.
4. **Community and care.** People help each other; the world is cooperative, not cutthroat. Even trade is neighbourly (§ Economy).

The one permanent boundary: **the everyday world never produces alarm or guilt** (`VISION.md §2.2`). Stakes exist only far away and long ago, or in the reserved future of combat — never on the doorstep.

---

## 3. Timeline of the world

Kept intentionally coarse — three broad ages, so specifics stay open.

| Age                           | What it was                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Elder Days** (ancient)  | The time of the First Tenders, who shaped the valleys and left the Old Works. Long ended; remembered only in fragments and ruins.             |
| **The Quiet Years** (between) | A long, uneventful stretch after the Elder Days faded — the world resting, the wild reclaiming the old places. Little is recorded, by design. |
| **The Tending Age** (now)     | The present. Ordinary folk farm the valleys, trade in small markets, and live simple, hopeful lives. The player arrives here.                 |

The player's own story begins at the start of the Tending Age's "today" — a newcomer taking up a plot of the good valley land.

---

## 4. Ancient history — the Elder Days

Long ago, a people called the **First Tenders** (or Elder Folk) lived in the valleys. They understood the land's giving nature deeply — some say they could _ask_ things of the soil. They raised the first stone works, terraced the hills, and set the pattern of field and furrow the world still farms by. Then, for reasons the present has forgotten, they passed — not in catastrophe as far as anyone remembers, but quietly, into legend.

What they left: the **Old Works** — worn stone foundations, mossed terraces, sealed doorways in the hills, and a scattering of odd, enduring artifacts. Most are harmless and half-buried. A few, in the deep places, are not yet understood. This is the seed of every future ruin, dungeon, and legendary item — kept vague on purpose (§ Consistency, § Story hooks).

---

## 5. The modern era — the Tending Age

Today the valley is a patchwork of small farms and smaller villages. Life is seasonal and neighbourly. People know each other; a market day is a social event as much as an economic one. There is no king pressing at the door, no war on the horizon — the modern world is small, safe, and local, exactly as the product needs it (`VISION.md §2.1`). Beyond the settled valleys lie the wilder edges the Tending folk rarely visit: the deep forest, the high mountains, the old mines, and the Old Works. These are the world's open frontier.

---

## 6. Origins of farming

Farming is the world's oldest kindness. The lore holds that the First Tenders learned the land's giving nature and taught it forward, so that even now a handful of seed becomes a harvest. Ordinary people do not need the Elder Folk's deeper understanding — the land gives to any patient hand. This is why the player, a newcomer with no special gift, can simply plant and be rewarded: **the world is generous by nature, not by privilege.** (Mechanically: `GAME_DESIGN.md §3` — crops grow and wait; nothing withers from neglect in v0.1.)

---

## 7. Origins of the villages

The villages grew where the land was kindest — beside good water and gentle soil, often near (but rarely on top of) an Old Works the founders half-understood and mostly left alone. Each village is small, self-reliant, and built from the timber and stone around it (`WORLD_BIBLE.md §2`). The starting region is such a place: an old, welcoming valley with room for a newcomer to take up a plot. Specific village and place names are **left open** for the town content of v0.3 to establish; when named, they become canon.

---

## 8. Natural laws of the world

The world runs on a few gentle rules, stated so future content stays physically consistent:

1. **The land gives.** Tended soil yields; growth completes and waits (`GAME_DESIGN.md §2.2, §3`). Decay is minimal and never destroys value (`GAME_DESIGN.md §12`).
2. **Seasons turn** (future, v0.2) in a steady, reliable cycle — the world is orderly and trustworthy.
3. **Wonder is quiet and rare.** Whatever the First Tenders knew is uncommon now; the everyday world is natural, not magical (§ Magic).
4. **Distance is age.** The further from the settled valleys, the older and less understood the land — the world's danger and mystery scale with distance and depth, never with time-at-home.

---

## 9. Magic (future)

Magic is **reserved, rare, and gentle.** In the Tending Age it is nearly absent — a folk memory of what the First Tenders could do. Future RPG content (v1.0) may awaken it in the deep places and old artifacts, but its register stays enchantment, not spectacle (`WORLD_BIBLE.md §8`; visual signature Rare Violet, `COLOR_PALETTE.md §4`). The lore keeps magic vague on purpose so that whatever v1.0 needs can be true. Rule: **magic never trivialises the cozy farming present** — it lives at the frontier and in the past, not in the everyday.

---

## 10. Technology level

As `WORLD_BIBLE.md §6`: pre-industrial and cozy-agrarian — hand tools, timber and stone, animal husbandry, a coin market. No machines, guns, or electricity. Future "advanced" systems (the v0.4 factory) are clever hand-built craft — waterwheels and timber gears — never industry. This file adds only the fiction: such craft is admired as ingenuity, in keeping with a people who value patient making.

---

## 11. Beliefs & the sense of the sacred

Beliefs are **light, folkish, and optional** — the world has no organised religion pressing on anyone. People hold gentle customs: a word of thanks to the land at planting, small tokens left at an Old Works "for luck," seasonal festivals that mark the turning year (§13). If any reverence exists, it is toward **the giving land itself** — a warm, unnamed gratitude rather than a doctrine. This keeps the world inclusive and cozy and leaves any deeper cosmology open for future content.

---

## 12. Important historical events

Deliberately **few and soft.** The world's history is not a chronicle of wars but of quiet turnings: the passing of the First Tenders, the long Quiet Years, the slow founding of the villages. No dated catastrophes are canonised, so future content can place its own events without collision. If a future session needs a historical event, it invents one consistent with §§1–8 and it becomes canon.

---

## 13. Festivals, traditions & food culture

The warm texture of everyday life — rich enough to flavour NPCs and quests, open enough to extend.

- **Festivals** follow the season (future, v0.2): a **Planting Day** in spring (hope, first seeds), a **Harvest Festival** in autumn (abundance, the year's reward), a quiet **Lantern Night** in winter (warmth against the dark). Festivals are gatherings, not obligations.
- **Local traditions:** market day as a social event; sharing the first fruit of a crop with a neighbour; leaving a small token at an Old Works.
- **Food culture:** hearty, simple, seasonal — bread from the wheat, stews and pies from the roots and pumpkins the player grows (`GAME_DESIGN.md §3.1` crops). Food is comfort and community; a shared meal is the world's default kindness.

Specific festival names/dates beyond the above are open for v0.2/v0.3 to fix.

---

## 14. Economy, trade & famous families (fiction)

The _numbers_ are `GAME_DESIGN.md §6`; the _story_ is here.

- **The economy is neighbourly.** Coins exist and prices move (`GAME_DESIGN.md §6.1, §6.2`), but trade is cooperative, not predatory — a fair exchange among people who know each other. There are no villains in the market.
- **Trade routes** are modest cart-paths between villages and, later, to the wider region (v0.3+). Nothing global; the world's commerce is local and human-scaled.
- **Famous families** are **left open** — a merchant lineage, an old farming family, a founder's line — to be named by the town content of v0.3. The bible fixes only that such families are ordinary and warm, not noble or scheming. When named, they become canon.
- **The merchant** the player sells to (v0.3) is a friendly, recurring face, not a faceless system — a person, drawn on the shared rig (`CHARACTER_BIBLE.md §1`).
- **The four villagers** (canon since phase-19, per §17 rule 2): **Marla** (the north-west cottage, fair-haired, often at the well), **Tobin** (the north-east cottage, fond of the castle walk), **Prue** (the south-west cottage), and **Edwin** (the south-east cottage, the elder of the four). Ordinary and warm, exactly as this section requires; surnames and family ties stay open for later content to fix.
- **Standing and the quest chains** (canon since phase-22): the town's regard for the player is spoken of in three ordinary words — **Newcomer**, **Friend of the town**, **Pillar of the town** — earned only by keeping promises, never lost. The notice board's chains are named **"A Good Neighbour"** (the town's) and **"<Name>'s Errands"** (one per villager). All of it neighbourly per this section: gratitude, not fealty, and no ledger of grudges — the town does not keep count of what went undone.

---

## 15. Legendary creatures, monsters & bosses (reserved)

The Tending Age has **no monsters at home** — the everyday world is safe (§2). What it has are _legends_: a great stag glimpsed in the deep forest, something old that stirs in the sealed Old Works, tales told at Lantern Night. These are the seeds, kept vague, from which v1.0 draws its creatures and bosses.

Rules for when they arrive (v1.0): monsters and bosses live at the **far edges and deep places**, never in the settled valleys; they are _mystery made manifest_, not gore or horror (`WORLD_BIBLE.md §9`); and their existence never retroactively makes the cozy present unsafe. A legend can wake in a dungeon; it cannot move into the village.

---

## 16. Mythology

The world's myth is a single, gentle shape: **the land gives, the Elder Folk understood it, the understanding faded, and something of it still sleeps in the old places.** That is the whole load-bearing myth — one open sentence. Every future legend, artifact, dungeon, and creature should be a _facet_ of it, consistent with it, never a contradiction of it. Keeping the myth this small is what lets a hundred sessions add to it without breaking it.

---

## 17. Future story hooks

Explicit, reusable seeds for future content — each consistent with the canon above:

- **The Old Works.** Sealed Elder-Folk sites in the hills and deep places → the ruins and dungeons of v1.0.
- **The faded understanding.** Whatever the First Tenders knew → the origin of future magic and legendary items.
- **The far frontier.** Forest, mountains, mine, and the wider map → v0.4 exploration and gathering.
- **The neighbours.** Villages, the market, and their families → v0.3 town, NPCs, and contracts.
- **The turning year.** Seasons and festivals → v0.2 living-world content and seasonal events.
- **The glimpsed legends.** Tales of great creatures → v1.0 monsters and bosses.

A future session picks a hook, develops it within the canon, and its development becomes canon.

---

## 18. Future expansion opportunities

The lore is shaped so the whole roadmap (`VISION.md §4`) fits without a retcon: v0.2's living world is the turning year and its festivals; v0.3's town is the neighbouring villages and their people; v0.4's exploration is the far frontier and its resources; v1.0's RPG, dungeons, and combat are the Old Works waking and the glimpsed legends made real. Each was seeded here in advance so it arrives as _fulfilment_, not as contradiction.

---

## 19. Consistency rules

**Binding on every future session that writes any fiction in this world.**

1. **Never contradict established lore** unless explicitly instructed. If a new story needs something this file already fixes, it obeys it. Changing fixed lore is a deliberate, recorded decision (a `LORE_BIBLE.md` edit), never a casual one.
2. **What is unstated is open — until stated.** This bible is deliberately light. A session may establish a new name, family, event, or place _consistent with §§1–18_; once written down, that addition **becomes canon** and binds later sessions the same way. Record such additions here.
3. **Tone is law.** Everyday content is warm, safe, optimistic, and low-stakes (`VISION.md §2.2`). Danger and mystery live only at the far edges, the deep places, and the reserved v1.0 future — never at home, never driven by player inattention.
4. **Distance and depth carry the stakes**, not time. Home is always safe; the frontier is where adventure lives (§8.4).
5. **Keep the myth small.** New legends are facets of the one open myth (§16), never rivals to it.
6. **Mechanics and product win.** If the fiction conflicts with `GAME_DESIGN.md` (numbers/mechanics) or `VISION.md` (tone/intent), those own their domains and the fiction bends. Story never overrides the product thesis.
