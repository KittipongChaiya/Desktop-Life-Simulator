# ADR-041: The Cozy Pass — Raising the Visual Craft Bar

**Status:** Accepted — v0.5 Phase 32.
**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Phase:** v0.5 Phase 32 (Art Direction & The Palette) — the foundation every later art phase draws on.
**Bound by (not re-litigated):** `STYLE_LOCK.md` (immutable except by a recorded project-level decision — this is that decision, and it amends exactly one rule); `ART_DIRECTION.md` (the stated intent, which does not change); ADR-006 (art is generated by deterministic scripts, and the script is the editable source); `VISION.md` §1 (the art is glanced at, not stared at); ADR-001 §1 and ADR-017 (render on demand; motion costs a lease).

---

## Context

The owner's judgement, and it is correct: the game reads as a **functional
pixel-art prototype** rather than a cozy world someone wants to leave open.

What makes this ADR interesting is that the stated direction was never wrong.
`ART_DIRECTION.md` §2 has said _"soft, warm pixel art with clean silhouettes"_
and _"cozy farm, made with care"_ since phase 05.5. The documents describe the
game the owner wants. **The assets never reached them.**

So the question is not _what should the art be_ — that was settled and is
unchanged. It is _why did five versions of asset production not get there_, and
the answer is in the code rather than in the documents.

### The look is a consequence of the drawing vocabulary

`scripts/lib/pixel-art.mjs` offers, in total: `set`, `fill`, `rect`, `ellipse`,
`outlineSilhouette`, `contactShadow`, and a seeded `prng`.

Every asset in the game is therefore a rectangle, an ellipse, or a union of the
two, wearing a uniform 1 px keyline. Reviewed on a contact sheet at gameplay
scale, the consequences are exact and mechanical:

- **The cottage, storage shed, mill, kitchen and market stall are the same
  building.** A trapezoid roof over a box, ±10 px. Their silhouettes are within
  a few pixels of each other, which is why phase-25 could ship two of them
  invisible and no test noticed the _shapes_ were interchangeable either.
- **The tree is a symmetrical green blob on a brown stick**, because
  `ellipse` makes symmetrical blobs and there is no other way to make a canopy.
- **Ground tiles are flat fills with scattered single pixels**, because there is
  no dithering, no ramp stepping, and no cluster brush — so "texture" can only
  be uncorrelated noise.
- **Everything is outlined identically**, because `outlineSilhouette` traces the
  whole alpha edge and R-04 requires it everywhere.

You cannot author _deliberate pixel clusters_, _strong shape language_, or
_subtle texture_ with that toolkit. The library's vocabulary is the ceiling, and
every asset has been pressed flat against it.

---

## Decision

**The direction is unchanged and the craft bar rises. Three things change: the
drawing vocabulary, the palette's warmth and breadth, and one locked rule.**

---

### 1. R-04 is amended: outlining becomes SELECTIVE

**Was:** _"Do NOT vary outline thickness. Exactly 1 px at 1×, on every entity
and object, everywhere."_

**Now:** _"Do NOT vary outline thickness — an outline is exactly 1 px where it
is drawn. Outline where an object must SEPARATE from what is behind it, not
uniformly. Interior edges, and edges already separated by value, take a darker
shade of the object's own colour instead of the outline ink."_

The original rule's stated reason survives and is why the 1 px part is kept:
variable line weight is the clearest tell of mixed-source art. What does not
survive is _everywhere_. A uniform keyline traced round every object is what
makes a scene read as **stickers laid on a background** rather than things
standing in a place — and it is the single largest contributor to the flat look
the owner is describing.

Selective outlining is how cozy pixel art gets depth without gradients (which
R-02 rightly forbids): the silhouette holds against the ground, and the
interior is modelled in the object's own ramp.

**This is the only STYLE_LOCK rule this ADR changes.** Every other rule agrees
with the new brief and is reaffirmed rather than touched — flat handcrafted art
(R-01), no gradients or soft alpha (R-02), no pure black (R-03), one perspective
(R-05), one light (R-06), one pixel density (R-15), and no detail that dies at
gameplay zoom (R-16).

### 2. The palette expands, warms, and gains the families it never had

R-08 stands: a colour outside `COLOR_PALETTE.md` is still forbidden. That file
is what changes, by the reviewed process R-08 requires.

The current 28 colours carry four greens, two woods, three stones, three waters
and a handful of accents. Against the brief they have three specific gaps:

| Gap                | Today                                       | Why it hurts                                                              |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cold stone**     | `#5C5A66` `#7D7A88` `#A6A2B0` — grey-violet | Reads sterile. The brief names "warm stone" and forbids grey environments |
| **One wood ramp**  | `#96704A` `#B58A5E`                         | Every wooden thing in the game is the same two browns                     |
| **No roof family** | Roofs are `STRAW`                           | Every building has the same straw roof, which is most of why they merge   |

Added: a warm stone ramp, a second and third wood, a roof family, creams, and a
small set of gentle flower accents. **Reserved accents are untouched** — R-09
still means Reward Gold signals reward, and a wider palette makes that easier to
hold, not harder.

### 3. The drawing library gains a vocabulary

The foundation everything else in v0.5's art phases is built on, and the reason
this ADR comes before any asset work:

| Primitive             | Buys                                                              |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ordered dither**    | Texture and value transition without gradients (R-02 safe)        |
| **Ramp shading**      | Hue-shifted steps, so shadow is cooler and light warmer than base |
| **Cluster brush**     | Organic, non-symmetrical masses — foliage that is not an ellipse  |
| **Polygon / line**    | Angular shape language — gables, fences, signs, tools             |
| **Pattern fill**      | Planks, thatch, shingles, stone courses — material identity       |
| **Selective outline** | §1's rule, as a function rather than a discipline                 |

**Ordered dither has a scale limit, found by prototyping and recorded in
`ART_DIRECTION.md` §9.2:** it textures small faces and transitions, and it
CANNOT texture a 32 px ground tile — a 4×4 matrix repeats eight times across
one tile and resolves into a visible cross-hatch. Ground texture is hand-placed
low-contrast clusters, and large-scale variation belongs to tile variants.

**None of these change what is legal.** Dithering is stepped, named colours;
ramps are palette entries; patterns are pixel placements. R-02 forbids smooth
gradients and soft alpha, and every primitive above is hard-edged and indexed.

### 4. Density is a rule now, not a preference

`ART_DIRECTION.md` §9 already asks for environmental storytelling. The brief
asks for substantially more, and for it to be **intentional rather than
uniform**. That is a hierarchy, and it is stated so it can be checked:

| Tier | What                                              | Must be                        |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1    | Workers, crops, interactable buildings, resources | Readable instantly, always     |
| 2    | Buildings, paths, fields, region structure        | Visible, never competing       |
| 3    | Flowers, grass tufts, props, ambient creatures    | Enriching, never noticed first |

**Tier 1 may never be lost inside Tier 3**, and that is the test a density pass
fails on — not "does it look full".

### 5. What this does NOT license

Written down because the pressure of a visual pass is to keep going:

- **No simulation, economy, save-format, worker-AI or production change.** A
  visual change may require a small supporting renderer change; it may not
  require a gameplay one. If it seems to, the visual idea is wrong.
- **No new render cost that survives idle.** ADR-017's two motion classes are
  unchanged: ambient motion stays off by default and surrenders the frame loop,
  and every finite effect still takes a lease. A cozy world that burns a core is
  not cozy — `VISION.md` §2.1 is the product.
- **No mixed pixel density**, no non-integer scaling, and no asset that is
  legible only above gameplay zoom. R-15 and R-16 are load-bearing for an
  overlay and this pass makes them _harder_ to hold, not softer.

---

## Consequences

**Good**

- The direction stops being aspirational. The documents said "cozy, made with
  care" for five versions; the pipeline could not express it, and now can.
- One amended rule rather than a rewritten style lock: the contract that has
  kept independently-authored assets coherent survives intact.
- The library upgrade is reusable. Every future asset — plugin art included —
  gets the better vocabulary for free.

**Bad, and accepted**

- **A large amount of existing art will be replaced.** The audit governs what,
  and assets that already fit are kept; but the honest expectation is that most
  of the world set is rebuilt.
- **Richer art is more code.** The generators grow. That is the price of a
  scripted pipeline, and it buys determinism, reviewable diffs, and no binary
  blobs of unknown provenance (ADR-006 §2).
- **Selective outlining is a judgement call**, where uniform outlining was a
  rule a script could enforce. Mitigated by the primitive making the common case
  the easy one, and by the contact-sheet review gate that already exists.

**Risky**

- **Density versus the idle budget.** More sprites on screen is more scene-graph
  work. §4's Tier 3 is static and holds no lease, so the cost is a one-time
  draw — but it is a real number and phase 39 measures it rather than assuming.
- **The reference image was not supplied.** This ADR is written from the
  owner's written brief, which is detailed and specific. If a reference arrives
  and disagrees, that is an amendment here.

---

## Revisit if

- An asset family genuinely needs a second pixel density → that is R-15, and it
  is a bigger decision than this ADR.
- The density pass measurably costs the idle budget → §5's constraint wins over
  §4's ambition, and the density comes back down.
